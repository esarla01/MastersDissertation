# Thesis figure style

The house style for every conceptual diagram in the thesis, so they all look like one family.
Use this whenever building, editing, or reviewing a figure, even if not asked, so nothing ships
in a one-off style. Work in `thesis/` only.

## The reference look
**Figure 1.1 (the manual review process) is the canonical reference** (Yasmin's favourite, 5 Jul
2026). Rounded cards with a soft shadow and a pale fill, a coloured bold title, plain body text,
and thin neutral-grey arrows. Figures 3.1 (scoping) and 3.2 (tool at a glance) follow it. When in
doubt, make the new figure look like Figure 1.1.

## Source of truth: `thesis/includes.tex`
The actual definitions live in the shared diagram block in `includes.tex` (added 5 Jul 2026).
**Reuse these; never redefine colours inline or duplicate the hex.** They are:

- **Colours:** `diagblue` / `diagbluefill` and `diagteal` / `diagtealfill` (roles per the two
  colour grammars in rule 1), `diagbody` (body text), `diagconnector` (arrows).
- **Card styles:** `diagio` (blue I/O card), `diagstage` (teal process card),
  `diagchipblue` / `diagchipteal` (small chips), all built on `diagbase` + `diagshadow`.
- **Arrow:** `diagarr` (thin, `>=latex`, neutral grey). **Label:** `diaglbl` (italic).
- **Figure-local accent:** `toolorange` (RGB 198,110,20) for content generated by the tool.

## The rules
1. **Semantic colour, not decoration.** Two legitimate colour grammars, each internally strict --
   pick ONE per figure:
   - **Exchange diagrams** (Figs 1.1, 3.3): blue = the client's side (what arrives), teal = the
     practice's side (the actor, and what it returns).
   - **Flow diagrams** (Fig 3.1 funnel, the pipeline): blue = input/output artefacts, teal = a
     process stage or activity.
   `toolorange` = tool-generated output. Grey = neutral/structural.
   **`toolorange` extended (ratified 24 Jul 2026, Fig 3.3 redesign):** orange also marks a
   *language-model call-point* — a step where the tool invokes the model. Same underlying idea (orange =
   where the model does the work); pair it with a legend entry naming it. The orange chip recipe for a
   call-point badge: `draw=toolorange!60, fill=toolorange!10` (a deliberate variant of the !45 border rule).
   One accent per role; never cycle colours like a rainbow.
   **Colour-vision safety (added 23 Jul 2026, per Nature guidance and Okabe-Ito):** never encode a
   distinction by red-vs-green alone -- pair colour with a label, position or fixed ordering. Test
   every figure by desaturating to greyscale: if it still reads, it survives colour-blind viewing
   and black-and-white printing. The Fig 3.2 risk chips pass only because the caption declares the
   colours illustrative of a scale the team configures.
2. **Cards:** use `diagio` / `diagstage`. Rounded corners, soft `diagshadow`, pale fill, a bold
   coloured title, `diagbody` text. Never a hard black drop shadow or a gradient.
   **Box vocabulary (codified 23 Jul 2026):** cards (`diagio`/`diagstage`) for primary elements;
   chips (`diagchipblue`/`diagchipteal`) for small secondary elements (template columns, tiny
   samples). The box recipe is: pale `<colour>fill` fill, `<colour>!45` border at 0.7pt, 5pt
   corners (chips: 0.6pt border, 3pt corners). A box drawn manually with `\draw` (e.g. Fig 3.2's
   input card, sized to match its table) must reuse exactly this recipe with the NAMED colours --
   no new hexes, no new geometry numbers.
   **Internal hairlines:** 0.4pt grey -- `black!15` for dividers inside a card, `black!25` for
   table grids.
   **Legend box:** `draw=black!22, fill=black!3`, 3pt corners, 7pt inner sep (Figs 3.2/3.3).
   **Annotation machinery** (the yellow scope brace, any future highlight mark): 1.1pt line, its
   own named accent (`scopeyl` #E6C229 marks the reviewed scope), always explained in the caption.
   An arrow that belongs to the annotation (the brace's arrow into the output, Fig 3.3) shares the
   annotation's colour and springs from its tip -- the one exception to rule 3's grey.
3. **Arrows:** always `diagarr` -- 0.9pt, grey `diagconnector`, `latex` tip, all baked into the
   style, so never restyle width or colour inline. **Arrows touch the borders of the boxes they
   connect, at both ends** -- no floating arrows, no gaps (agreed 22 Jul 2026; watch nodes with
   `inner sep`, which pushes the drawn border away from the anchor). `diagarr, dashed` is the one
   sanctioned modifier and means an exception or retry path, never primary flow. Arrow labels use
   `diaglbl` (serif italic), kept to a few words, starting with a capital (rule 6).
4. **Type and size:** `\sffamily` throughout (baked into the `diag*` styles), sentence-style labels,
   and only the title is bold. **Box titles are coloured to match the card's accent** (diagblue title
   on a `diagio` card, diagteal on a `diagstage` card), as Figures 1.1 and 3.1 do; Figure 1.1's
   `\large` titles are the hero-diagram exception, standard cards keep the title at (or one step
   above) body size.
   **TikZ font gotcha (cause of a serif/sans mix found 22 Jul 2026):** a node or style option
   `font=\scriptsize` REPLACES the inherited `font=\sffamily`, it does not compose. So every
   `font=` in a figure must spell the family out: `font=\sffamily\scriptsize`, never a bare size.
   Audit any new figure for bare `font=\<size>` before shipping.
   **Wrap gotcha (found 23 Jul 2026, Fig 3.1):** in some nodes `text width` sizes the box but
   fails to govern the paragraph's wrap point (root cause unresolved). Never trust the wrap:
   render and check where lines break, and when it misbehaves take control with manual `\\`
   breaks and size the box to the resulting lines.
   **Hard floor:** no rendered text below 7pt (measure per the pdfminer rule below); the dense-figure
   band of 7.4-7.6pt is the accepted practical minimum, not a target. Aim for a consistent nominal size across figures -- house default:
   title `\footnotesize\bfseries`, body one step down (`\scriptsize` in dense figures, `\footnotesize`
   in sparse ones). Keep box text short, since detail belongs in the caption.
   **Rescaling caveat (the important part):** every figure is scaled by `\resizebox`, so the *rendered*
   text size depends on the figure's natural width and its scale, not on the nominal `\footnotesize` /
   `\scriptsize`. Matched nominal sizes do not guarantee matched rendered sizes -- a dense figure scaled
   down renders smaller text than a sparse one. So the real test is to render the figures, eyeball the
   body text side by side, and tune until the text reads at the same size across every figure.
   Three levers, in order of preference: the picture's NATURAL WIDTH (narrower boxes = more
   upscale -- this is how Fig 3.1 was matched to 1.1's 9.3pt on 23 Jul 2026), the resize target,
   and only then the nominal sizes.
5. **Caption:** states the takeaway, not just a label (the house prose rule applies to captions).
   House caption shape: *noun-phrase title, colon, one-breath takeaway* (Figs 1.1, 3.1, 3.2).
   **Self-containment:** figure plus caption must stand alone without the body text -- expand any
   abbreviation visible in the figure, and always declare illustrative/synthetic content.
6. **Title/body separator inside a box (agreed 21-22 Jul 2026):** wide single-flow boxes run the
   bold title into the text with a colon ("Broad, open-ended ask: ..."); stacked cards put the body
   under the title with a small vertical gap (`\\[4pt]`) and NO colon.
   **Capitalisation (simplified 23 Jul 2026, supersedes the earlier continuation carve-out):**
   every free-standing text element starts with a capital letter -- box bodies, numbered items,
   arrow labels, legend entries, faded context lines. Only two exceptions: (i) text running on
   after an inline title's colon ("Broad, open-ended ask: “an AI tool...”"), and (ii) verbatim
   quoted content, which keeps its source casing (Fig 3.2's blue extracted wording -- blue MEANS
   verbatim, so its casing is data, not style).
7. **Legend swatches:** always `\legendswatch[<width>]{<colour>}` from `includes.tex` -- its lift is
   computed to centre on the text x-height. Never hand-nudge swatch alignment with `yshift`.
8. **Colour = meaning, nothing else.** One accent per role, every accent explained (legend or
   caption); document text in at most two neutral grey shades (content vs faded context). A new
   colour needs a new *role*, not a new place to decorate. An established colour never
   takes a new role in a later figure: `toolorange` means tool-generated output everywhere, forever.
9. **Verify rendered sizes by measurement, not eyeballing:** extract per-character effective sizes
   with pdfminer (`LTChar.size` on the figure's page) -- this includes the `\resizebox` scaling and
   settles size disputes instantly. **The target is single: every figure's body text renders at
   8.3pt (+/-0.2), Figure 1.1's measured size. Density is not an excuse**
   *(Re-baselined 24 Jul 2026 after the outer margin moved to 2cm, widening the text block to
   16.5cm; earlier baselines: 8.0pt at 11pt/16cm, 9.3pt at 12pt/16cm. If class or geometry change
   again, re-measure Figure 1.1 and re-tune the others to it.)* -- Fig 3.3 hit 9.0pt on
   23 Jul 2026 after its text went a step up and its card geometry was respaced. A figure that
   cannot reach the band is redesigned (split, narrowed, decluttered), not exempted.
10. **Fit to width:** wrap the picture in `\resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{...}` by default. The governing
   test is the **rendered** size (rule 4), not the nominal target, so the target is a lever, not a
   fixed rule: a sparse figure may sit at `0.95\textwidth` on purpose to stop its few words rendering
   oversized (Figs 1.1 and 3.1 do this), while a dense figure uses full `\textwidth`. Pick the target
   that makes the rendered body text match the family, then render and eyeball to confirm.

   **Known residual (Jul 2026):** Figure 3.2 renders about a step smaller than the rest because it is
   by far the densest (a document plus a four-column table). Enlarging its text cleanly is not possible
   without more width -- which `\resizebox` then scales back down -- or dropping content, so its slightly
   smaller text is accepted as inherent to the figure. The other four conceptual figures are uniform.

11. **Raster content:** diagrams are always TikZ (vector). Unavoidable rasters, such as the
    Chapter 4 UI screenshots, go in at 300 dpi or better, never upscaled, and pass
    `confidentiality-check` first.
12. **Decoration budget:** the `diagshadow` blur is the house's one decorative signature. Nothing
    else ornamental -- no gradients, no 3D, no decorative borders. If an element carries no
    information and is not the shadow, cut it (Rougier et al.'s "avoid chartjunk", PLOS Comput
    Biol 2014).

13. **External box labels and file-format tags:** a label sitting above a card ("Input (.docx)")
    is `\sffamily\footnotesize\bfseries`, coloured per the figure's colour grammar (rule 1), with
    the file format as an upright `\texttt` tag in neutral `black!80` -- never italic (agreed
    22 Jul 2026, Fig 3.3).
14. **Document facsimile and embedded tables:** when a card depicts a document (Fig 3.3's input),
    the vocabulary is: bold masthead, plain subtitle, a 0.4pt `black!15` hairline under them, and
    faded `black!40` ellipsis lines ("Earlier sections...") for out-of-scope content -- two grey
    shades only (rule 8). A table embedded in a figure gets `black!25` 0.4pt grid lines, NO outer
    border, bold sans headers centred in their columns, and `\arraystretch` raised (~2.35) so rows
    breathe; verbatim cell content keeps its source casing (rule 6). Text columns in an embedded table are
   RAGGED-RIGHT (`>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}m{...}`), never justified -- justification in
   narrow columns stretches word gaps and forces hyphens, clashing with the card text beside it.
15. **Geometry of siblings and pairs:** boxes at the same level share dimensions (Fig 3.1's four
    act boxes) and keep a visible gap -- never touching. Paired input/output elements share height,
    tied programmatically (Fig 3.3 draws the card's rectangle to the table's bottom edge with
    `|-`), never by matching hand-tuned constants that drift apart. Narrow cards suppress
    hyphenation (`\hyphenpenalty=10000` in the style) and break lines manually instead.

## Data charts (evaluation plots, added 23 Jul 2026)
Chapter 5's plots follow the same family rules: `\sffamily`-matching sans text, the 7pt rendered
floor, and colour with meaning. Additionally:
- **Series colours:** draw qualitative series from the colour-blind-safe Okabe-Ito set
  (#0072B2 blue, #009E73 green, #E69F00 orange, #D55E00 vermillion, #CC79A7 purple, #56B4E9 sky,
  #F0E442 yellow, #000000 black), keeping diagblue/diagteal roles where a chart echoes a diagram.
- **Never** rainbow/jet colormaps; sequential data takes a single-hue ramp, diverging data a
  two-hue ramp through a neutral midpoint.
- **Direct labels beat legends** where space allows (label the line, not a key box).
- Greyscale test applies to every chart before it ships.

## Documented exceptions (Figure 3.2, the tool at a glance)
- The **input "document"** uses the standard `diagio` card, kept uniform with the family (Yasmin,
  5 Jul 2026), with the in-scope section shown as a **raised white panel** (white fill, `diagblue`
  border, `diagshadow`) so it stands out on the pale-blue card.
- The **output is a table**, not a card (it shows structured data). Its provenance is colour-coded:
  clause + wording in `diagblue` (extracted verbatim by the tool), review comment in `toolorange`
  (generated by the tool), and risk as a **customisable colour chip** set by the human. Those risk
  colours are illustrative and deliberately *not* from the shared palette, because the real risk
  scale is fully user-configurable.

## Build recipe
1. Reuse the `diag*` styles from `includes.tex`; add only a figure-local accent if genuinely needed.
2. Design fast first if it helps (an SVG mock via the visualize tool), then translate to TikZ.
3. Blue cards for input/output, teal for process/actors, `diagarr` for flow.
4. **Always render the figure's PDF page and eyeball it before shipping.** TikZ overlaps
   (legend-on-table, label-on-box) compile without error, so a clean build is not proof the figure
   is right. Find the page from `main.aux` (`\newlabel{fig:...}{{n}{page}}`; the PDF page index is
   the printed page plus the front-matter offset), then read that page.
5. Hand off to `thesis-ship` to build, check, and sync -- and it is edited in parallel in Overleaf,
   so always pull-rebase before pushing.

## Pipeline-diagram conventions (settled Jul 2026, Figs 3.4/3.5)
These are the rules that make the extraction and retrieval pipeline figures read as one family.
Apply them to any new process/pipeline diagram.

- **Always-on vs conditional LLM calls.** An LLM call that fires on *every* run is drawn with a
  faded diagonal hatch over its orange box (`postaction={pattern={Lines[angle=45, distance=8pt,
  line width=0.6pt]}, pattern color=toolorange!32}`); a conditional call is a plain orange box.
  The legend groups both under one **`LLM call:`** heading -- plain swatch "fires only when
  triggered", hatched swatch "fires on every run" (legend swatch uses `distance=4pt`, `!42`).
  Do **not** write timing text ("on every schedule") inside a box when only one instance exists --
  let the hatch carry it.
- **Box wording.** The non-bold body must *continue* the bold coloured title so the two read as one
  phrase (title "Assign a topic", body "from the 21-topic taxonomy"). Never introduce a bare term
  the reader has not been given (ground every cold reference, e.g. say what "topic" is).
- **Data-feed vs flow arrows.** A persistent store feeding a step (not passing control to it) uses a
  **distinct dashed arrow in the same amethyst arrow colour** (`diagarr, dashed`), with its own
  legend entry "Data feed" -- never a new colour. Encode "feeds data" through the *arrow style*, not
  the box colour.
- **Semantic colour is fixed; do not invent colours.** A store is a blue I/O card ("Input / output
  & store"). Do not recolour a data source (e.g. pink) to make it stand out -- pink (RGB 255,20,147)
  is reserved for review flags and would clash. One accent per role only.
- **Grey "zone" frames group a cluster, not a singleton.** The framed, titled grey zones
  ("Deterministic backbone" / "LLM calls") earn their place in Fig 3.4 because many LLM calls sit in
  a column that contrasts with the deterministic spine. Do **not** add a zone around a *single*
  inline LLM call (Fig 3.5) -- the orange fill + hatch + legend already identify it, and a one-box
  zone re-creates the "conditional side-branch" misread we removed.
- **Sizing a tall figure: tighten pitch, don't uniform-shrink.** `\resizebox` scaling shrinks the
  text as well, so to reduce height while keeping text readable (matched to the sibling figure),
  reduce the *vertical pitch between rows*. Watch the binding constraint: a parallel column of boxes
  (e.g. the five orange LLM boxes) sets a minimum pitch for the rows beside it, so use **variable
  pitch** -- tighten only the gaps with no side-box (top and bottom), keep the flanked middle at its
  natural pitch, then re-space the side column to clear gaps. Render and eyeball for collisions.
- **Chevron/handoff strips.** For a left-to-right actor handoff (Fig 3.3), draw explicit flat-left
  arrow-block pentagons and centre each label in the *body* rectangle (not the shape centre, which
  the point skews), with bold point edges. Do not rely on the `signal` shape's auto-centring.
- **Ampersand in box labels (Yasmin, Jul 2026).** Inside a diagram box, use `\&` rather than the
  word "and" to keep labels compact -- e.g. "clause reference \& wording", "dense \& lexical search",
  "Extract clauses \& wording". This applies to the boxes only. Captions and subcaptions keep the
  word "and" and read as normal prose; the ampersand is a space-saving device for the diagram itself.

## UI screenshots (a separate family from the diagrams above)

Everything above governs the drawn TikZ diagrams. Screenshots of the running tool follow their own
rules, settled 9 Aug 2026 across Figures 4.1, 4.2, 4.3 and D.1. Apply these to any new capture.

### Every panel gets a hairline frame
A screenshot on a white background has no edge and dissolves into the page, which is unreadable once
a figure has several panels. So every screenshot panel is framed:

```latex
{\setlength{\fboxsep}{2pt}\setlength{\fboxrule}{0.4pt}%
\begin{figure}...
\fcolorbox{black!30}{white}{\includegraphics[width=...]{figures/...}}
...\end{figure}
}%
```
Scope the two lengths in a group around the figure so no other box in the thesis is affected. Where an
image fills its subfigure, trim the width by 5pt (`width=\dimexpr\textwidth-5pt\relax`) to leave room
for the frame, or it overflows.

### Sizing: rendered text size is source pixels divided by physical width
This is the rule that governs everything else, and matching pixel widths by eye does **not** work.
Compute the fraction from the capture's native resolution:

| Family | Density | Renders at | Used by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Close-up crops | ~1,370 source px per full `\textwidth` | ~1.5 mm text, readable | Figure 4.2's six panels |
| Whole-screen captures | fixed at `0.65\textwidth` | ~0.7 mm text, below legibility | Figures 4.1, 4.3, D.1 |

So a 645 px crop sits at `645/1372 = 0.470\textwidth`, a 732 px crop at `0.534`, and so on.

Whole-screen captures are **deliberately** below legibility. They carry page structure, not readable
detail, and the readable detail lives in the close-up crops and the prose. The default is
**0.65\textwidth** (Figures 4.1 and 4.3). **Appendix D.1 sits at 0.52** instead, because fitting two
panels beneath the appendix bullets needs that and it keeps the appendix to two pages (Yasmin, 9 Aug
2026). Page count wins over matching sizes when the two conflict, since an appendix exists to show the
interface exists rather than to be read closely.

### Crop from the full-resolution view, never a shrunk window
A crop taken from a zoomed-out browser window carries too few pixels and renders illegibly whatever
width it is given. A 224 px attempt at the tooltip would have rendered at about 0.4 mm. Aim for
roughly 600 px or more for a close-up.

### Pairing panels side by side
Only adjacent panels, so the a-f order still matches whatever table refers to them, and only where the
two widths sum to under 1.0 with a little slack. A panel wider than about 0.66 cannot pair with
anything. Use `[b]` alignment so the subcaptions line up rather than the image tops.

### Captions and subcaptions
- House form: **what it is, then "The data shown is illustrative and synthetic."** Nothing else.
- Per-panel notes only where the panels show genuinely different things that the subcaptions cannot
  carry. Appendix D.1 keeps its notes for that reason. Figures 4.1, 4.2 and 4.3 have none.
- Subcaptions repeat the referring table's wording **exactly**, so a reader moving between the table
  and the figure sees the same name.

### Splitting a figure across pages
A float is one unbreakable box, so a tall figure cannot use a gap at the foot of the previous page.
Split it into two `figure` environments with `\ContinuedFloat` on the second, which keeps the number
and continues the subfigure lettering. Figure 4.2 and Appendix D.1 both do this.

## Confidentiality
A figure that shows client data, a real schedule, or a tool screenshot goes through
`confidentiality-check` first. Mark illustrative content as *synthetic* in the caption (as
Figure 3.2 does). The hard line is client-specific information.
