\section{Objective}

The chapter asks two questions of the same capability value, and each is
followed by whether an instruction repairs what it finds.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Q1 Construction.} When no source states the opening, can the
        model build it from the object's dimensions and its resting face, and
        which instructions help it do so?
  \item \textbf{Q2 Precedence.} When the text states a resting face and the
        image shows a different one, which source governs the opening the model
        derives, and can an instruction change which source wins?
\end{enumerate}

Q1 is answered under \texttt{congruent}, \texttt{congruent\_face} and
\texttt{dims}, and its repair under the instruction configurations of
Section~\ref{sec:ex2-steps}. Q2 is answered under \texttt{conflict} and
\texttt{conflict\_face}, and its repair under the same configurations together
with the two precedence directives. The two questions are reported separately
because they concern different failures and have different repairs.

\subsection{The decision as a sequence of steps}
\label{sec:ex2-steps}

An allocation in this cell requires a sequence of inferences, not a single
judgement. The conditions and the instruction configurations both index that
sequence, from opposite ends: withholding a field \emph{adds} a step the model
must perform, and an instruction factor \emph{scaffolds} one it would otherwise
perform unaided.

\begin{table}[h]
\caption{The steps an allocation requires, what adds each one, and what
scaffolds it.}
\label{tab:ex2-steps}
\centering
\begin{tabular}{clll}
\toprule
 & The model must & Added by & Scaffolded by \\
\midrule
0 & recognise the stated size is a convention & the \texttt{named} frame
  & nothing \\
1 & read the resting face from the image & \texttt{dims} & A, attention \\
2 & map that face and the dimensions to an opening
  & \texttt{congruent\_face} & C, derivation \\
3 & compare that opening with each aperture & every condition & none \\
\midrule
\multicolumn{2}{l}{state step 2's result before committing to step 3}
  & & D, elicitation \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Two properties of this table matter before the conditions are given.

Step~0 has no instruction factor and cannot have one. A sentence telling the
model that the stated size is a convention would be a fact about the prompt
rather than about the scene, and the boundary rule that separates the ladder
from the directives forbids it. The \texttt{extents} frame therefore
\emph{removes} step~0 rather than scaffolding it, which is why it is reported
as a control in Section~\ref{sec:ex2-frame} and does not appear among the
configurations below.

Precedence is not a step. It is a second axis, concerning which source supplies
step~1's input when the text and the image disagree. The conflict conditions do
not lengthen the sequence; they contest one of its inputs.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Constructing the opening}

Can the model build the required opening when no source states it? Because
construction, unlike retrieval, can fail partway, the question is answered one
step at a time.

\subsubsection{A supplied opening is applied correctly}

When \texttt{opening\_needed\_m} is supplied, all three models apply the
constraint correctly. They select a Franka on 100\,\% of \texttt{small\_face}
trials and 0\,\% of \texttt{large\_face} trials at all 32 positions, with no
waits, and every reply reports the correct opening.

Step~3 is therefore not the source of any failure below. Any shortfall arises
where the opening must be produced, not where it must be used.

\subsubsection{Mapping a stated face to an opening}

Under \texttt{congruent\_face} the resting face is stated but the opening is
withheld, so the model must map \texttt{small\_face} to a 0.050\,m opening and
\texttt{large\_face} to a 0.100\,m opening before applying the capability rule.

The models differ sharply in this step. Gemini performs the mapping without
error, producing a complete face-dependent allocation at all 32 positions. GPT
reaches an 89.6-point contrast $[84.1, 95.0]$. Claude does not recover the
mapping at all: its contrast is $-5.2$ $[-21.6, 11.2]$.

\textbf{Claude's failure therefore occurs before visual grounding.} Because it
cannot consistently derive the opening from a stated face, its \texttt{dims}
result cannot establish whether it can derive that quantity from the image, and
every Claude figure below sits downstream of this.

\subsubsection{Deriving the opening from the scene}

Under \texttt{dims} both the face and the opening are withheld, so all three
steps run in one decision. Gemini separates the two faces by 46.9 points
$[34.5, 59.3]$, GPT by 2.1 $[-6.2, 10.3]$ and Claude by $-10.4$ $[-24.3, 3.5]$.
Only Gemini's interval excludes zero.

performance on each step in isolation.

\textbf{The steps are individually available and they do not compose.} GPT
reads the pose at 89\,\%, maps a stated face at 89.6, applies a supplied
opening at 100, and scores 2.1 when all three run in one decision. Gemini
passes every step without error and still loses more than half its contrast.

The cue-validation figure is drawn from a separate probe of 20 scene pairs
rather than from the 32 analysed positions, so it is reported as converging
evidence rather than as a fourth measurement on the same sample.

\subsubsection{The result disappears without the image}

Although Gemini's positive contrast under \texttt{dims} suggests visual
inference, it could also have been produced by an unforeseen textual cue. To
test this, the condition was rerun with the image withheld and every other
input unchanged.

Without the image none of the three models shows a meaningful difference
between the two faces. Gemini's contrast falls from 46.9 to $-6.2$
$[-14.3, 1.8]$, and all three intervals span zero. The contrast Gemini achieves
under \texttt{dims} is therefore visual.

The result does not show whether a model ignores the image or looks at it and
fails to use the information consistently.

\textbf{Attention is inert alone and worth 20.8 points on top of derivation and
elicitation.} At \texttt{N-A} the attention sentence buys GPT nothing, moving
its contrast from 2.1 to 2.1. At \texttt{N-ACD} the same sentence, added to
derivation and elicitation, produces a gain whose interval excludes zero. The
factors therefore interact, and a ladder that reports each in isolation
understates what instruction can achieve.

\texttt{N-order} stays flat for every model, so \texttt{N-D}'s effect is
attributable to its wording rather than to the reordering of the response
fields it also imposes.

\texttt{N-ACD} was added after the six pre-registered configurations had been
run. It is not part of the pre-registration, and
\texttt{prompts.PRE\_REGISTERED\_LADDER} records which six are.

\subsubsection{What the frame was contributing}
\label{sec:ex2-frame}

The \texttt{dims} glossary states the object's extents as
\texttt{size\_upright\_m}, measured standing on its smallest face. The block's
smallest face is the 0.100\,m by 0.050\,m face, so that phrase names the
\texttt{small\_face} condition exactly. A model reading the convention as a
description of the current pose would answer \texttt{small\_face} on every
trial without consulting the image, and would be indistinguishable from a model
that cannot read the pose at all.

The \texttt{extents} frame states the same three numbers as
\texttt{extents\_m}, largest first, and names no orientation. It was run at
three points on the instruction axis: the baseline, the strongest
pre-registered configuration, and the complete procedure.

\textbf{Neither frame is neutral.} The \texttt{named} frame anchors the answer
to 0.050\,m and the \texttt{extents} frame anchors it just as firmly to
0.100\,m. GPT moves from reporting 0.050 on 93.8\,\% of baseline trials to
3.6\,\%, and Claude from 100\,\% to 4.2\,\% at \texttt{N-CD}. Both baselines
produce a contrast near zero because both produce a constant answer, differing
only in which constant.

What separates the two frames is what each convention asserts. Standing on its
smallest face asserts a \emph{pose}, which competes directly with what the
image shows and continues to pull after the model is instructed: GPT reaches
only 66.1\,\% face accuracy at \texttt{N-CD} under this frame. Three extents
largest first asserts only an \emph{ordering}, which contradicts nothing and is
abandoned once the model is told the opening depends on the resting face:
95.8\,\% under the same configuration.

The frame effect is not additive. It is worth 47.9 points to Gemini at the
baseline and nothing once instructed, because Gemini saturates. It is worth
nothing to GPT at the baseline and between 35 and 44 points once instructed,
because a frame cannot assist a model that is not attempting the derivation.
Claude is unmoved throughout: its answer follows whichever convention the
prompt carries, and its face accuracy remains at chance under both.

Four configurations were not run under \texttt{extents}. The interaction is
therefore characterised at three points on the instruction axis rather than
seven, and this section cannot identify which factor breaks the anchor.


\subsection{Source precedence}

When the text states one resting face and the image shows another, which source
governs the opening the model derives?

\subsubsection{A supplied opening is followed despite the contradiction}

Under \texttt{conflict} all three models reverse the allocation completely. The
contrast is $-100.0$ at all 32 positions for every model, against a matched
\texttt{congruent} ceiling of $+100.0$. Every one of the 576 replies reports
the opening supplied by the text rather than the one implied by the image.

A false declared opening therefore produces an exactly inverted allocation with
the correct scene in view, and the contradicting image produces no measurable
change.

\subsubsection{The text still governs when the opening is absent}

Under \texttt{conflict\_face} the opening is withheld, so the model must derive
it from a face, and the face described in the text is false. GPT's contrast is
$-89.6$, matching its $+89.6$ under \texttt{congruent\_face}, and Gemini's is
$-100.0$, matching its $+100.0$. Both therefore follow the face stated in the
text rather than the face shown in the image.

Claude's contrast is 7.3 $[-6.7, 21.3]$ and spans zero, as it does under
\texttt{congruent\_face}. Given its failure in Section~5.6.1.2 to derive the
opening from a stated face, there is no clear evidence of precedence for it in
either direction.

\subsubsection{Whether instruction changes which source wins}

The six instruction configurations were run in \texttt{conflict\_face} as well
as in \texttt{dims}.

\textbf{The ladder does not move precedence.} Gemini is unmoved at every

Claude becomes worse, by a margin whose interval excludes zero. The derivation
instruction takes it from 7.3 to $-66.7$, a change of $-74.0$
$[-89.7, -58.2]$. Teaching a model to derive an opening from a stated face,
when the stated face is false, converts an incoherent allocator into a reliably
inverted one. A repair aimed at the wrong step does harm.

\subsubsection{Naming the source explicitly}

The two precedence directives add one sentence to \texttt{N-A}. Both name the
image and they differ in a single word, so a movement toward the scene under
one and not the other cannot be a response to image-talk.

One sentence repairs Gemini completely. It moves from following the text on
every trial to following the image on every trial, and \texttt{X-state} leaves
it unmoved, so the effect is attributable to which source was named.

Two cautions follow the numbers rather than the other way round.

GPT's $-1.6$ is not a repair. Its image-following rises from 0\,\% to 49\,\% of
informative trials, which is chance. A contrast near zero here means no
systematic bias in either direction, not correctness: the model obeyed an
instruction it had no perception to execute. Its score improved by 88 points
while its use of the scene did not.

The directive is also not free. Applied to \texttt{congruent\_face}, where the
text and the image agree and there is nothing to arbitrate, the same sentence
costs GPT 22.4 points, from 89.6 to 67.2, with an interval excluding zero.
Gemini is unaffected at 100.0. In deployment the presence of a conflict is not
known in advance, so the directive cannot be applied selectively.

The conflict conditions were not run under the \texttt{extents} frame. Where
the resting face is stated, the field is specific and the glossary convention
is general, and the models follow the stated face on close to every trial, so
the frame is unlikely to bear on precedence. That is an argument rather than a
measurement, and it is declared as such.
